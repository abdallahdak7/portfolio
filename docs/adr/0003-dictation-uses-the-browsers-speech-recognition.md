# Dictation uses the browser's speech recognition

Dictation turns speech into text with the browser's own `SpeechRecognition` (`webkitSpeechRecognition` in Chrome and Safari) rather than recording audio and sending it to a transcription model through Tom's Worker. That keeps the runtime dependencies to React and the icon set, adds no backend work or cost, and needs no audio upload of our own. The cost is reach and control: Firefox has no speech recognition, so the microphone button simply isn't shown there, and in Chrome the browser sends the audio to Google to transcribe, which the button's tooltip tells the Visitor. Dictation only ever fills the draft, never sends, because recognition gets names and technical terms wrong and every Visitor turn counts against the Conversation's limit. Moving to server-side transcription would mean a new Worker endpoint and Turnstile-gated audio uploads; revisit if Firefox reach or transcription quality starts to matter.
